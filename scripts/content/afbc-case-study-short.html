TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

High Bed Temperature in an AFBC Boiler

An AFBC boiler designed for 850–900°C was running at around 950°C at rated load. When the load was reduced, the temperature came down.

What we found: the immersed bed coils couldn't remove enough heat at full load. The effective coil immersion was 792 m against a required 946 m, a shortfall of about 16%.

Common causes of poor bed-coil heat pickup:

  • Low expanded bed height
  • Excess refractory covering the coils
  • Eroded or incorrect stud pattern
  • Change in fuel quality or coal–char mix
  • Uneven combustion-air distribution

Key takeaway: increasing airflow alone may not fix high bed temperature. Heat-transfer surface, refractory, studs, fuel and air distribution have to be checked together, and any fix should be backed by a verified heat-transfer calculation.
